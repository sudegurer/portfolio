Research

Adaptive Deep Tensor Networks for Function-on-Function Regression

In preparation

Gürer, S., Bakıcıerler Sezer, G., Beyaztaş, U. — TÜBİTAK-funded project (Scholarship Researcher) · forms the basis of ongoing MSc thesis, Marmara University

A deep learning architecture that predicts an entire response curve from multiple functional and scalar predictors. Instead of representing predictors with a fixed basis such as B-splines or FPCA, AdaDTN learns its own basis functions end-to-end — each one a small neural network — so the representation adapts to whatever part of the signal actually matters for prediction. The model outputs interpretable coefficient surfaces and distribution-free prediction bands via conformal calibration, and comes with formal approximation and generalization guarantees. In simulation, it holds its own against classical and fixed-basis methods when the signal is smooth, and pulls ahead when the true effect is sharply localized — exactly where a generic, response-agnostic basis struggles.

Longitudinal Functional Joint Models for Repeated Functional, Longitudinal, and Time-to-Event Data

In preparation

Gürer, S., Mutis, M., Beyaztaş, U.

Most joint models that use imaging data lean on a single baseline scan and throw away everything measured afterward. This project keeps the full picture: a repeatedly observed functional predictor is split into a stable, subject-level trend and a visit-specific fluctuation, each linked to a longitudinal clinical outcome and, through a shared trajectory, to a time-to-event endpoint. Applied to PET imaging and cognitive-decline data from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), the model recovers the imaging effect and the induced conversion-risk more accurately than baseline-only approaches, particularly when the imaging signal genuinely evolves over follow-up.

Locally Sparse and Robust Partial Least Squares in Scalar-on-Function Regression

Published · 2024

Gürer, S., Shang, H.L., Mandal, A., Beyaztaş, U. — Statistics and Computing

A partial least squares method for regressing a scalar outcome on a functional predictor that stays reliable when the data contain outliers and automatically identifies which regions of the predictor curve actually drive the outcome — improving both prediction accuracy and interpretability over standard PLS. Implemented in the accompanying robsfplsr R package. Presented at the 5th International Applied Statistics Congress (UYIK), Istanbul, 2024.
